# Domain 1: Information Systems Auditing Process (18%)

The mechanics of planning, executing, and reporting an IS audit — the "how to audit" domain.

## 1.1 Audit standards, guidelines, and codes of ethics

- **ISACA IS Audit and Assurance Standards** — mandatory requirements for CISA holders; **Guidelines** provide guidance on applying the standards; **Tools and Techniques** are supporting/optional reference material.
- **ISACA Code of Professional Ethics** — requires objectivity, due professional care, confidentiality, and acting in a manner that reflects positively on the profession.
- **Independence**: **organizational independence** (the audit function reports to a level — e.g., the audit committee/board — high enough to be free of undue influence from the areas it audits) vs. **individual/professional independence** (the specific auditor has no personal conflict of interest, financial interest, or close relationship with the auditee).

## 1.2 Risk-based IS audit planning

- **Audit universe** — the complete inventory of auditable entities/processes across the organization.
- **Risk-based planning** — prioritizes audit engagements toward areas of highest risk (likelihood × impact) rather than auditing everything equally or on a fixed rotation regardless of risk.
- **Audit charter** — a high-level document approved by the board/audit committee establishing the audit function's purpose, authority, and responsibility.
- **Long-term/annual audit plan** — derived from the audit universe and risk assessment, resourced against available audit staff/budget.

## 1.3 Audit project management

- **Engagement planning**: defining scope, objectives, and criteria for a specific audit before fieldwork begins.
- **Audit program** — the detailed set of procedures/steps to be performed to achieve the audit objectives.
- **Resource/schedule management** for the engagement; **materiality** — the threshold at which an omission or misstatement would influence the judgment of someone relying on the audit results.

## 1.4 Types of audits and assurance engagements

- **Compliance audit** — verifies adherence to a specific law/regulation/standard.
- **Operational audit** — evaluates efficiency/effectiveness of operations.
- **Financial audit** — focused on the accuracy of financial statements/records (often the domain of financial auditors, but IS auditors support the IT-dependent controls within it).
- **Integrated audit** — combines operational, financial, and IT/IS audit perspectives.
- **Forensic audit** — investigates a specific suspected fraud or irregularity.

## 1.5 Audit evidence

- **Evidence reliability hierarchy** (generally, strongest to weakest): evidence obtained directly by the auditor (e.g., observation, re-performance) > evidence obtained from independent third parties > evidence obtained from the auditee's own records/representations.
- **Evidence types**: physical/observational, documentary, testimonial (interviews), analytical (recalculation, trend analysis).
- **Sufficiency and appropriateness** — evidence must be both adequate in quantity (sufficient) and relevant/reliable (appropriate) to support the audit conclusion.

## 1.6 Sampling methodologies

- **Statistical sampling** — uses probability theory, allows the auditor to quantify sampling risk (e.g., random sampling, systematic sampling).
- **Non-statistical (judgmental) sampling** — based on auditor judgment, no quantifiable confidence level.
- **Attribute sampling** — tests a control's compliance rate (yes/no — did the control operate?) — used for **compliance testing**.
- **Variable sampling** — estimates a numeric value or amount (e.g., total dollar value of errors) — used for **substantive testing**.
- **Sampling risk** — the risk that the sample isn't representative of the population, leading to an incorrect conclusion.

## 1.7 Reporting and communication of audit results

- **Audit report structure**: typically scope/objectives, methodology, findings, risk rating, recommendations, and management's response/action plan.
- **Findings** should include criteria (what should be), condition (what is), cause, and effect/risk — and a recommendation.
- Reports should be communicated to the appropriate level (management responsible for the area, and the audit committee/board for significant findings).
- **Follow-up** — auditors should track whether management's agreed-upon corrective actions were actually implemented, not just documented as a commitment.

## 1.8 Quality assurance and improvement of the audit function

- **Quality Assurance and Improvement Program (QAIP)** — includes both ongoing internal monitoring and periodic **external quality assessments** (peer review, typically every 5 years for internal audit functions following IIA standards) to confirm the audit function itself operates per applicable standards.

## Key exam tips

- CISA questions frequently test "what should the auditor do FIRST" — usually risk assessment/planning steps come before fieldwork/testing steps.
- Know attribute sampling (compliance testing, pass/fail) vs. variable sampling (substantive testing, dollar amounts) — a frequently tested distinction.
- Organizational independence (where the audit function reports) is distinct from individual independence (a specific auditor's personal conflicts) — both are tested separately.
