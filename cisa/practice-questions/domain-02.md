# Domain 2 Practice Questions: Governance and Management of IT

Original questions written for self-study, aligned to the domain outline in [`../domains/02-governance-and-management-of-it.md`](../domains/02-governance-and-management-of-it.md). Not sourced from any official exam.

**1.** The board approves the organization's overall risk appetite and monitors whether IT is delivering strategic value, while the CIO's team executes day-to-day IT operations within that direction. This distinction best illustrates:

A) Governance vs. management
B) Segregation of duties
C) Application vs. general controls
D) Risk transfer

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Governance (board/executive level) sets direction, approves risk appetite, and monitors performance/compliance; management (operational level) executes within that direction. This is one of the most frequently tested conceptual distinctions in this domain.
</details>

---

**2.** Which ISACA framework is most directly aligned with the CISA exam's approach to IT governance and management, distinguishing governance processes (Evaluate, Direct, Monitor) from management processes (Plan, Build, Run, Monitor)?

A) ITIL
B) COBIT
C) TOGAF
D) PRINCE2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** COBIT is ISACA's own governance/management framework and explicitly separates governance objectives from management objectives — directly relevant background for CISA.
</details>

---

**3.** A developer at a mid-size company also has the ability to deploy their own code directly to the production environment without independent review. This is primarily a failure of:

A) Risk appetite definition
B) Segregation of duties
C) Portfolio management
D) Enterprise architecture

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** A developer having both development AND production deployment access without independent review is a classic segregation-of-duties conflict — one of the most commonly cited IT governance findings.
</details>

---

**4.** In the "three lines" model, which line provides independent assurance directly to the board regarding the effectiveness of risk management and controls?

A) First line (operational management)
B) Second line (risk management/compliance functions)
C) Third line (internal audit)
D) External regulators

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Internal audit constitutes the third line, providing independent, objective assurance to the board/audit committee — distinct from the first line (who own and manage risk directly) and the second line (who oversee and support risk management).
</details>

---

**5.** An organization sets its overall willingness to accept risk in pursuit of its objectives. This is known as:

A) Risk tolerance
B) Risk appetite
C) Inherent risk
D) Residual risk

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Risk appetite is the overall amount of risk an organization is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives, typically set at the board level. Risk tolerance is the acceptable variation around that appetite.
</details>

---

**6.** Which framework is more operationally/service-delivery focused (incident, problem, and change management processes) compared to COBIT's broader governance focus?

A) ISO 31000
B) ITIL
C) NIST CSF
D) COSO ERM

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** ITIL provides detailed IT service management processes (incident, problem, change management) and is more operationally focused than COBIT, which addresses governance and management holistically at a higher level.
</details>

**7.** An IT steering committee's primary function is to:

A) Perform hands-on system administration
B) Prioritize IT investments and align them with business strategy across functional areas
C) Conduct the annual financial audit
D) Approve individual employee access requests

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The IT steering committee, composed of business and IT leadership, prioritizes and aligns IT investment decisions with overall business strategy — a governance-level function, not an operational one.
</details>

**8.** A CISO reports directly to the CIO, whose department's projects and systems the CISO's team is responsible for securing and, at times, flagging risk concerns about. What governance issue does this reporting structure potentially raise?

A) None — this is always the optimal structure
B) A potential conflict of interest that could compromise the security function's independence and influence
C) A segregation-of-duties conflict identical to a developer with production access
D) A failure of enterprise architecture

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** When the CISO reports to the CIO, there's a potential conflict of interest — the CISO may be inclined to soften security concerns about projects their own boss champions. Auditors commonly flag this reporting line as a governance risk worth evaluating (though not automatically a finding — it depends on compensating controls, such as a dotted line to the board).
</details>

**9.** Which of the following best describes "enterprise architecture" in an IT governance context?

A) The physical layout of a data center
B) A structured blueprint of an organization's IT assets, processes, and their relationships, used to guide strategic IT decisions
C) A synonym for the IT department's org chart
D) A synonym for the network diagram

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Enterprise architecture is a structured, strategic-level blueprint connecting business processes, applications, data, and technology — used to guide IT investment and design decisions in alignment with strategy.
</details>

**10.** Which statement best distinguishes a program from a project in IT portfolio management?

A) A program and a project are identical terms
B) A program is a single project with a longer timeline
C) A program is a coordinated group of related projects managed together to achieve benefits not achievable by managing them individually
D) A project always contains multiple programs

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** A program coordinates multiple related projects to realize benefits (synergies, shared resources, aligned outcomes) that wouldn't be achieved by managing each project in isolation.
</details>
