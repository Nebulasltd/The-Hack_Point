# Domain 1 Practice Questions: Information Systems Auditing Process

Original questions written for self-study, aligned to the domain outline in [`../domains/01-information-systems-auditing-process.md`](../domains/01-information-systems-auditing-process.md). Not sourced from any official exam.

---

**1.** An organization's audit function reports directly to the audit committee of the board, rather than to the CFO whose department it frequently audits. This primarily demonstrates:

A) Individual auditor independence
B) Organizational independence
C) Due professional care
D) Risk-based planning

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Organizational independence concerns where the audit function sits in the reporting structure — reporting to the board/audit committee, above the areas it audits, preserves this independence. Individual independence concerns a specific auditor's personal conflicts.
</details>

---

**2.** Which approach to audit planning prioritizes engagements toward the areas of highest likelihood and impact, rather than auditing every area on a fixed rotating schedule regardless of current risk?

A) Compliance-based planning
B) Risk-based audit planning
C) Statistical sampling
D) Substantive testing

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Risk-based audit planning directs limited audit resources toward the areas of the audit universe that currently represent the highest risk, rather than treating all areas equally.
</details>

---

**3.** An IS auditor wants to test whether a specific control (e.g., "all changes require manager approval") was consistently applied across a population of 10,000 changes. Which sampling approach is most appropriate?

A) Variable sampling
B) Attribute sampling
C) Judgmental sampling only
D) No sampling — 100% review is always required

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Attribute sampling tests a yes/no compliance question (did the control operate or not) across a sample and is the standard approach for compliance testing of a control's operation.
</details>

---

**4.** Which type of evidence is generally considered MOST reliable for an IS auditor?

A) A written representation from the auditee describing their own process
B) Evidence obtained directly by the auditor through independent observation or re-performance
C) A verbal assurance from IT management that the control works
D) A policy document stating the control should exist

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Evidence the auditor obtains directly and independently (observing the control operate, or re-performing it) is generally the most reliable, ranking above the auditee's own representations or documentation stating intent.
</details>

---

**5.** After an audit report is issued with agreed management action plans, what should the IS auditor do next?

A) Consider the engagement closed once the report is issued
B) Perform follow-up to verify the agreed corrective actions were actually implemented
C) Immediately begin the next audit in the plan with no further action on this one
D) Delegate all follow-up responsibility to the auditee with no independent verification

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Follow-up is a required part of the audit process — the auditor should verify whether agreed-upon corrective actions were genuinely implemented, not simply accept management's commitment at face value.
</details>

---

**6.** A finding in an audit report should generally include which components?

A) Only the recommendation
B) Criteria, condition, cause, effect, and recommendation
C) Only the auditee's response
D) A list of every control tested, regardless of outcome

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** A well-formed finding states the criteria (what should be), the condition (what actually is), the cause, the effect/risk, and a recommendation — giving management everything needed to understand and act on the issue.
</details>

---

**7.** Which document, approved by the board or audit committee, establishes the audit function's purpose, authority, and responsibility?

A) Audit program
B) Audit charter
C) Engagement letter
D) Management representation letter

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The audit charter is the foundational, board-approved document defining the audit function's mandate, scope of authority, and reporting relationship.
</details>

---

**8.** An IS auditor is testing a sample of transactions to estimate the total dollar value of errors in a population. Which sampling approach applies?

A) Attribute sampling
B) Variable sampling
C) Non-probabilistic sampling only
D) Sampling is never appropriate for dollar-value estimation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Variable sampling is used to estimate a numeric value (such as a total dollar amount of error) across a population — the technique associated with substantive testing.
</details>

---

**9.** Which term describes the threshold at which an omission or misstatement in the information being audited would be likely to influence the judgment of someone relying on the results?

A) Sampling risk
B) Materiality
C) Residual risk
D) Inherent risk

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Materiality is the threshold of significance — an issue below the materiality threshold generally would not change a reasonable decision-maker's judgment, while one above it would.
</details>

---

**10.** An external quality assessment (peer review) of the internal audit function itself is an example of:

A) A compliance audit of a business unit
B) The audit function's own Quality Assurance and Improvement Program (QAIP)
C) A forensic audit
D) Substantive testing

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** A QAIP includes both ongoing internal monitoring and periodic external quality assessments to confirm the audit function itself is operating per applicable professional standards.
</details>
